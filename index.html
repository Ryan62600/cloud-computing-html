Cloud Computing

Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services, such as storage, servers, databases, and software, over the internet instead of relying on a local computer or a physical server in your own building. Rather than installing everything on your own device, you access these resources from providers over the internet, usually paying only for what you use.

One of the biggest advantages of cloud computing is flexibility. Users can scale their storage or processing power up or down depending on their needs, access their files from anywhere with an internet connection, and avoid the cost of maintaining their own hardware. This is why many companies and even individual students, like myself, rely on cloud services for storing files, hosting websites, and running applications.

Cloud Services I Know

Google Drive

Google Drive is a cloud storage service that lets users upload, store, and share files such as documents, photos, and videos. It also allows multiple people to view and edit files like Google Docs and Sheets at the same time.

Google Classroom

Google Classroom is a cloud-based platform used by schools to manage classes online. Teachers can post assignments and announcements, while students can submit their work and view grades from any device with internet access.

Amazon Web Services (AWS)

AWS is a cloud computing platform that provides services such as servers, storage, and databases for businesses and developers. It is used to host websites, run applications, and store large amounts of data without needing physical hardware.
